PathCheck: three design directions

Each direction shows the same three pages with the same content from your design brief. Open them on a phone and on a desktop, in light and in dark mode. The student is made up, and most buttons don't work yet. Nothing here is approved until you say so.

A. Receipt

Start Check my courses See what I have left

Why this direction

The harm in this problem is paying for a course that doesn't count. So the result is drawn as the thing people already check before they pay: a receipt. Courses are line items on a strip of paper with credits in a mono face, and the total sits where a total sits. Students who are anxious about money read a receipt without being taught how. Page 3 is the matching statement: 121 total, minus 27 counted, minus 13 in progress, leaves 81. The type is IBM Plex Sans with IBM Plex Mono for codes and figures, whose zero is dotted so MGT 201 can't be misread.

How page 2 makes "Doesn't count" impossible to miss

Three ways at once. The two courses are named in a ruled box before the list starts ("2 don't count: BIO 230, ENG 140"). In the list, each is a voided line: a hatched band with a heavy edge that no other row has. And each carries the octagon-with-a-cross mark and the words in bold. It reads as a line struck from a receipt, which is information, not an alarm.

How page 3 makes "what's left" obvious on a phone

"81 credits left" is the heading and "On pace for Spring 2029" is the line under it, so both are on the first screen. The subtraction beside it shows where 81 comes from without a chart to decode.

B. Meter

Start Check my courses See what I have left

Why this direction

The fear behind the problem is graduating late, which is a question of distance. This direction draws the degree as one bar, 121 credits long, and shows where the student is on it. Solid is counted, striped is being taken now, open is still to do, and each part has its number and its word written on it, so there is no key to look up. The same bar returns smaller on every requirement. The type is Atkinson Hyperlegible, a face drawn so that look-alike characters differ, in one weight pair for everything: friendly for a first-year, and course codes stay unambiguous.

How page 2 makes "Doesn't count" impossible to miss

The strip at the top has one cell per course. Four are striped; two are crossed out and labelled "Doesn't count" with the course code, so the answer is visible before reading a single row. Below, those two rows are the only tinted, outlined ones on the page, with the octagon mark and larger words. The other rows never say "counts" without the condition under it.

How page 3 makes "what's left" obvious on a phone

The first screen is the number, the pace, and the bar. The open part of the bar is by far the longest, and it is labelled "81 left". A student sees how far there is to go before reading anything else.

C. Signs

Start Check my courses See what I have left

Why this direction

Students use this for a minute, on a phone, during registration week, and nobody reads instructions. Road signs are built for exactly that: a shape and a few large words, understood at a glance. Each answer here is a sign. The type is Barlow, a family drawn from highway signage, set large and condensed, with JetBrains Mono for course codes. The page is black on white with almost no color, so it prints cleanly when an advisor wants a copy. On a desktop the signs line up as a table that two people can scan together.

How page 2 makes "Doesn't count" impossible to miss

It is the only inverted thing on the page. A black band at the top names the two courses, and their rows are black bands too, with the octagon mark and the largest words in the list. It doesn't rely on color at all, and there is no red: it reads as a sign, not a warning light.

How page 3 makes "what's left" obvious on a phone

"81 credits left" fills the first screen in the largest type in the design, with the pace answer directly under it. Each requirement then leads with a large shape: a solid disc for Done, a half disc for In progress, an empty ring for Still needed.

What all three take from Apple's guidelines

  • Liquid Glass is only on the things that float above the page: the bar at the top, the bar at the bottom, and the suggestion menu. The content itself is never glass, and glass is never stacked on glass.
  • The page scrolls under the bars.
  • Buttons are capsule-shaped and at least 44 pixels tall. Each screen has one tinted action; on Start the two ways in are equal.
  • Every status has a word and its own shape: open circle with a tick (Counts), octagon with a cross (Doesn't count), solid disc (Done), half disc (In progress), dashed ring (Still needed).
  • If the device has Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast on, the bars turn solid. With Reduce Motion on, the "How it works" demo doesn't move.
  • Apple doesn't allow its own fonts and icons to be embedded on a website, so each direction ships open-licence fonts and draws its own marks.

Questions I could not resolve from the brief

  1. "15 count toward CIS." The four courses marked as counting are 3 credits each, which is 12. Fifteen would need BIO 230 or ENG 140 to count. The mockups show 12. Which is right?
  2. The condition on the count line. I added "unless you've already passed them or filled their slots" beside "12 count toward CIS", so the summary never promises more than the rows do. Keep it?
  3. Credit notes at 18 credits. Neither note applies at exactly 18. The mockups show both as quiet lines under the total. In the app, should a note appear only when it applies?
  4. "2 don't count: BIO 230, ENG 140." I added this line at the top of page 2 in all three directions. It isn't in the brief. Keep it?
  5. Done blocks. The brief names no courses for Orientation, Composition and Science & math, so they show only the name, credits and "Done". In the app, should a Done block open to list its courses?
  6. The suggestion example. Typing "mgt 3" suggests MGT 321, which is already in the list. In the app, should a course that is already added still be suggested?
  7. "Open choice". I used this tag on the plan's pick and elective entries to show they aren't real courses. Is the wording right?
  8. The bar labels. The top bar says "Check courses" and "What's left" to fit a phone. The Start page uses the full names. Acceptable?
  9. The demo. "How it works" is three still frames that fade in turn, with a pause control. Do you want that, a recorded video, or nothing?

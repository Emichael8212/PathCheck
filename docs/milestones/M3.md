## 2. The previews: a choice made at the launch

**Decided on 2026-11-18: the first way.** You set "Private" with "Applies to: Previews
only" and saved it in the same act that made the site public. Two previews, and the
address of the function on a preview, answer 401 and ask for a login (part 4, steps 6
and 7). What follows is the choice as it was put to you.

Every pull request gets a preview site. Before the launch only you could open one. On a
project that is public as a whole, previews are public too, and their addresses can be
guessed.

On a preview the bot check lets everyone through, because previews use Cloudflare's test
keys. The three-a-day limit does not hold there either. And previews share the month's
$5 count with the real site. So someone could spend the month's $5 on a preview, and the
real site would then refuse every student until the next month. The cost stays at $5.
The service does not.

Any one of these closes it. The choice was yours.

1. **Keep previews private while the site is public.** Chosen. Netlify's documentation
   did not say whether the Free plan has the setting; its form has it.
2. **Remove the Gemini key from previews.** Previews then refuse every upload before
   anything is spent. A later change to the transcript reader cannot be tried on a
   preview unless you put the key back for a while. This is my recommendation if the
   first is not offered.
3. **Stop building previews.** You lose the preview link in each pull request.
4. **Change the code** so that a preview without your own Cloudflare keys refuses
   uploads. One more pull request.

## 3. What is kept, and where

PathCheck itself stores no personal data. This is everything that is kept anywhere, by
anyone, when a student uses the site.

| Where | What | Who can see it | How it goes away |
|---|---|---|---|
| The student's browser | The courses they typed; their last result, as the rows they checked, without passing letter grades; their "legacy student" answer; a random number for the day | Only that browser | "Clear my data", or clearing the browser's data |
| PathCheck's counter on Netlify | For each day and random number: how many uploads. For the month: how much was spent. Numbers only: no name, no file, no course | You, in Netlify | A day's counts are deleted at the first upload of a later day. With no uploads they stay |
| The function's log on Netlify | One fixed line when an upload is refused or fails, such as "read-transcript: refused (day-limit)". Nothing from a transcript | You, in Netlify | Netlify deletes its logs by its own rules |
| The PDF | PathCheck never stores it. It is passed to Google to be read | Google | See Google, below |

**What others keep. These are not PathCheck's. You accepted them as they are on 2026-11-18:**

- **Google** reads the PDF and may keep it briefly. The upload control says so.
- **Cloudflare** runs the bot check in the student's browser when a file is chosen. It
  sees the visitor's browser. It does not get the PDF.
- **Netlify** keeps its own logs of requests, as every web host does. Since the launch it
  also adds its "Powered by Netlify" badge to every page, from the site's own address. In
  my read of 2026-11-18 the badge set no cookie and stored nothing in the browser: not
  when the page opened, not after its "Close" button was pressed, not after the page was
  opened again.
- **Your mailbox.** A report by email shows the student's email address and what their
  page showed: their courses and PathCheck's answers. It arrives only when a student
  chooses to send it. How long you keep such emails is your choice. Your screenshot of
  2026-11-18 shows that the mailbox forwards its mail to another address of yours, so a
  report is kept in both mailboxes.

By your answer of 2026-11-18 the promise reads "PathCheck stores no personal data". The
brief and the feature list now say it in those words.

### The browser, key by key

The app writes these and no other, and it sets no cookie (`STORED_KEYS` in
`src/remember.ts`; `tests/remember.test.ts` states the three keys and that no cookie is
set).

| Key | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `pathcheck.visit` | The list typed on "Check my courses" and its term. The last result: the rows the student checked, each with its course, title, term and credits. A passing letter grade is kept only as "Passed"; D, F, W, I and "in progress" are kept as they are. Rows that still need fixing are kept as they were read. The pace settings, and the plan's moves |
| `pathcheck.legacy` | The answer to "Legacy CIS student": Yes or No |
| `pathcheck.browser` | The day's random number, which counts the three uploads. On the hosted site only |
| `pathcheck.probe` | Nothing: written and removed at once, to see whether the browser can keep anything |

### Every line the function can write to its log

Two, each with one word from a fixed list (`netlify/functions/read-transcript.mts`).
Netlify shows them marked "ERROR".

- `read-transcript: refused (…)` with one of: `check`, `browser`, `day-limit`,
  `month-cap`.
- `read-transcript: unavailable (…)` with one of: `body`, `no-key`, `unreachable`,
  `timeout`, `refused`, `blocked`, `cut-off`, `not-rows`, `check`, `store`. Google's
  status number may follow, as in `unavailable (refused 503)`.

Nothing else is written. An upload that is read writes no line at all. Your screenshot
of 2026-11-18 shows exactly this: one "refused (day-limit)", and Netlify's own lines of
duration and memory.

Proof in the code: `tests/remember.test.ts` ("keeps only what the result needs", "sends
nothing anywhere", "clears everything"), `tests/limits.test.ts`,
`tests/read-transcript.test.ts`.

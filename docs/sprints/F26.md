# F26 — Public site live, with the owner's contact email and last curriculum review date

Milestone: M3 (its last feature) · Branch: `feat/F26-public-site` · Brief sections: §4 "On
every result", §7 (Hosting; Tests and real data), §9 (Safe; Live), §10 (M3), §12

## What the student gets
PathCheck at a public address: `https://pathcheck.netlify.app`, open to anyone without a
login. The foot of every page shows the contact address, `pathcheck.report@gmail.com`.
It is a link that opens an email already filled in with what the page showed, and it
reaches a real mailbox. Everything else is what the owner checked at M2.

## User-visible change
One, by the owner's answer of 2026-11-18: at the foot of every page the words "Report a
mistake" give way to the address itself, which is the link. Visual design is graded on
that line of the foot (1440 and 375 wide, light and dark). Clarity is graded on the foot,
the M3 record and the owner's steps.

## Who does what
By the owner's word of 2026-11-17: "Stop before anything goes public. I make the site
public and run the production deploy myself." So:
- **The agent** puts the owner's address in its one place, with its tests; writes the
  owner's steps and the M3 record; and after the owner has made the site public, reads
  the public pages (nothing is sent: no upload, no form) to confirm what is served.
- **The owner** gives the address; builds and publishes; makes the final check; makes the
  site public; and confirms the public site with one upload.
- **The evaluator** judges from the records, the owner's reports, and its own look at the
  public pages without an upload.

## What is built

### 1. The contact address, shown as the link
- `REPORT_ADDRESS` in `src/wording.ts` becomes `pathcheck.report@gmail.com`. It is the
  one place in `src/`.
- **The foot of every page** (`foot` in `src/shell.ts`): the link's words are the
  address and nothing else. `REPORT_A_MISTAKE` is deleted. The link opens the same email
  as before, to the new address. Its place, size and colour are the old link's. The
  address is never broken in the middle of a line.
- **The address can be copied** (the owner: "stays visible as text so it can be
  copied"): the link's words can be selected like any text, and the link's address up to
  its "?" is exactly `mailto:` and the words shown, so that a browser's "Copy email
  address" gives the address alone.
- **What keeps the words "report a mistake":** the subject of the email from the Start
  page, "PathCheck: report a mistake". It is not a label on a page. It tells the owner
  what the email is.
- **A screen reader** hears the address as the link's name, and nothing added (the owner:
  no label). Listed for the owner as the agent's note, not changed.
- **On paper** the link is not printed, as before (F17). Whether the address should be
  printed is listed for the owner.
- **The mockups** (`mockups/data.js`, `mockups/view.js`) show the same line with the same
  address: "Use this address everywhere". `docs/design/DESIGN.md` says that the owner
  changed this line on 2026-11-18.
- `scripts/check-build.mjs` refuses a built site that holds the placeholder's domain
  (`pathcheck.example`), so that a public build can never carry it.
- Tests: the link's words and address on every page (`tests/trust.test.ts`,
  `tests/start.test.ts`); the words "Report a mistake" on no page; the address written
  in one file of `src/`; the placeholder in no file of `src/` or `mockups/`; the link
  not printed (`tests/print.test.ts`); the build check refuses the placeholder
  (`tests/upload.test.ts`).

### 3. `docs/milestones/M3.md`, the record of the M3 check
As `docs/milestones/M2.md` is for M2: what M3 is; the owner's steps, each with what it
should show; what was seen; everything still open; every merged pull request of M3. The
owner's steps, in this order:
1. **The address.** The owner creates the mailbox and tells the agent the address. It
   will be readable by everyone, on the site and in the repository: a mailbox kept for
   PathCheck alone is wise.
2. **Build and publish**, as at M2, after the pull request with the address is merged
   (the site is still Private).
3. **A report reaches the mailbox.** On the private site the owner clicks the address at
   the foot of "Check my courses" with a course in the list, sends the email it opens,
   and finds it in the mailbox, with the page's lines in it.
4. **The handover guide's clicks, once** (carried forward from F25): the owner follows
   Step 2A of `docs/CURRICULUM-HANDOVER.md` with a later date up to the green check, says
   whether every label was as the guide writes it, and then **closes the pull request
   without merging and deletes its branch** (the review date stays 2026-11-15; the site
   is still Private, so that pull request's preview is not public).
5. **The final check** on the private site: both flows on a computer and on a phone, as at
   M2, with nothing in the browser's console from PathCheck's own files.
6. **Before the launch: the previews.** Today a Deploy Preview has the Gemini key and uses
   Cloudflare's test secret, so its bot check lets every caller through; the number of a
   browser is whatever the caller sends; and the previews count their uploads in the
   same store as the real site (`getStore`, the project's own, in
   `netlify/functions/read-transcript.mts`). That was safe while only the owner could
   open a preview. On a public project a preview's address can be guessed (the
   repository is public). Someone could then spend the month's $5 there, and the real
   site would refuse every student until the next month. The cost is capped; the service
   is not. One of these closes it, and **the choice is the owner's**:
   - **(1) Previews stay private.** Netlify's documentation says "Previews stay private
     unless you change the preview visibility setting", and does not say whether the Free
     plan has that setting. The owner looks at Netlify's visibility form at the launch:
     if it lets production be public while previews stay private, that is chosen, and
     nothing else is needed.
   - **(2) No Gemini key on previews** (the agent's recommendation if (1) is not
     offered): the owner removes the Deploy Previews value of `GEMINI_API_KEY`. A
     preview then refuses every upload at its key check, before anything is counted or
     spent, with "Upload isn’t available right now — try again tomorrow". The price: a
     later change to the reader cannot be tried on a preview unless the owner puts the
     value back for a while, and that preview is then public.
   - **(3) No previews at all:** Netlify can stop building Deploy Previews. The owner
     then has no preview to look at in a pull request.
   - **(4) A change of the code:** a preview without the owner's own Turnstile secret
     refuses uploads (no test secret). It ends uploads on previews for good and is a new
     pull request with its tests.
   Also read by the owner there: "Untrusted deploys policy" (Project configuration >
   Developer settings > Continuous deployment), which Netlify's documentation says is
   "Require approval" by default for a public repository, so that a pull request from a
   stranger's copy is not built with the secrets before the owner approves it.
7. **Launch:** the owner makes the site public (Netlify's visibility) and, if they wish,
   unlocks auto publishing; then a later merge publishes by itself. Neither is done by
   the agent.
8. **The public site:** in a browser that is not logged in to Netlify, on a computer and
   on a phone: Start opens; "Check my courses" answers a course; a made-up transcript is
   read; the fourth upload of a day is refused; the address at the foot opens an email to
   itself, and on the phone a long press on it offers to copy it. And a screenshot of Netlify's function log after that upload.

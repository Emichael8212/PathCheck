# F04 — Placing courses into requirement blocks: no double counting, electives, and the sequence rule

Milestone: M1 · Branch: `feat/F04-placing-courses` · Brief sections: §5 (the block table,
"No double counting", "Electives", the sequence rule) · Owner answers: 2026-11-15 "answers
on PR #1" items 2, 3 and 5 (rule-matched electives; two courses for one pick; the
sequence); 2026-11-16 "Transfer, AP and CLEP rows"

## What the student gets
Nothing visible yet. This is the rule that takes the courses a student has passed or is
taking and says which requirement each one fills, which courses fill nothing and why, how
many credits are counted, in progress and left, and whether the history or literature
sequence is complete. The checklist (F13), the answers with a transcript (F12), the pace
(F14) and the plan (F16) are built on it.

## User-visible change
No. Visual design and Clarity are N/A. Every check below names its automated test in
`tests/placement.test.ts`, run with `npm test`, using made-up students only.

## What is built
- `src/placement.ts`: `placeCourses(standings, curriculum)`. It takes the per-course
  results of F03 (`courseStandings`) and the curriculum (F02) and returns:
  - for each block: every required course as done, in progress or needed; every pick group
    with the course or courses that fill it and how many picks are still open; the block's
    credits done, in progress and needed;
  - for the CIS electives: the courses that fill the three places, how many are open, and
    the credits;
  - the sequence: done, in progress or needed, and for each alternative which courses are
    missing;
  - the courses that were passed or are in progress but fill nothing, each with a reason;
  - the totals: credits done, in progress and remaining out of the degree's credits.
- Only a course that F03 calls "passed" or "in progress" can fill a place. A passed course
  fills it as **done**; a course in progress fills it as **in progress** (it is assumed to
  pass, so the place is taken and the course will not be scheduled again, but it is never
  done). "Must repeat", "no credit" and "unrecognised" fill nothing: the requirement stays
  needed.
- Transfer, AP and CLEP credit arrives from F03 as "passed" and is placed like any passed
  course (owner answer of 2026-11-16).
- Credits always come from the curriculum, never from a transcript row: a listed course is
  worth its listed credits, and an elective is worth the elective credits per course.
- **Reasons a passed or in-progress course fills nothing.** A closed set of four:
  - *not in the curriculum*: in no list, and not matching the elective rule;
  - *pick already filled*: with the code of each course that fills it;
  - *electives full*;
  - *legacy-only elective*: it is one of the legacy-only courses; whether it can count
    depends on the legacy answer, which is F05. Never "not in the curriculum".
- **Transcript order** means the order `courseStandings` returns: the order in which each
  course first appears, even when that first row is a D that was later retaken.
- Not here: the legacy answer and legacy electives, including whether PSY 251 goes to its
  pick or to an elective place (F05); courses typed without a transcript (F08); the words
  Done / In progress / Still needed for a whole block, and anything on screen (F13).

## How "place it where it helps most" is read (recorded in `docs/decision-log.md`)
The brief says each course fills exactly one requirement and is placed where it helps
most. In this curriculum a course is only ever listed in one place (the one exception,
PSY 251, is F05's), so the choices that exist are which of several courses gets a place
that only some of them can have:

1. **A pick group** is filled by as many courses as it asks for. If the student has more,
   they are placed in this order:
   - a **passed** course before one **in progress**;
   - then, between two in the same state, a course that belongs to a **sequence**
     (HIS 132, ENG 210) before one that does not, because the owner said "HIS 132 fills the
     Social sciences pick; ENG 210 fills the Humanities pick";
   - then **transcript order**.
   The others fill nothing: "pick already filled".
2. **The three elective places** are filled passed before in progress, then in transcript
   order. A fourth elective fills nothing: "electives full".
3. **The sequence is judged on the courses themselves.** An alternative is done when both
   its courses are passed, and in progress when both are passed or in progress. This
   matters in one case: HIS 132 in progress while GEO 206, already passed, holds the pick.
   HIS 132 then fills nothing, but the history sequence is in progress, not "still needed".

Passed before in progress is the only order that counts a passed course that the table
lists for a place. The sequence and transcript-order tie-breaks only change which of two
interchangeable courses is named. None of the three changes the credits counted or
remaining.

**"Not already required"** in the elective rule is read as "not listed in any block,
whether required or a pick". For this curriculum it makes no difference, because no pick
course matches the elective rule (a test asserts that). If a future change to the data
makes one match, the reading matters and goes to the owner.

## Acceptance checks
1. **Required courses.** A passed required course is done in its own block; one in
   progress is in progress there; a required course with no row, or with only "must
   repeat", "no credit" or "unrecognised" rows, is needed. — "places a passed required
   course in its block", "keeps a course that was not passed as needed".
2. **One course, one place.** Whatever the transcript: no course fills more than one place;
   a pick group holds at most `choose` courses; only a "passed" or "in progress" course
   fills anything; a course that is not "passed" is never done; and whenever the sequence
   is done, both courses of the alternative that completes it fill a place. Checked on a
   student who passes every course listed in a block, and on a few hundred transcripts drawn at random
   with a fixed seed. — "never counts a course twice".
3. **A pick is filled by one course.** ENG 131 and ENG 140 both passed: the first on the
   transcript fills the first Composition pick; the other fills nothing, with the reason
   "pick already filled" and the code of the course that fills it. The second Composition
   pick (ENG 132 / ENG 141) is still open. — "fills a pick with one course only".
4. **Passed before in progress.** ENG 140 in progress and ENG 131 passed, in either order
   on the transcript: ENG 131 fills the pick as done; ENG 140 fills nothing and adds no
   in-progress credits. Two in the same state: the first on the transcript fills it.
   — "places a passed course before one in progress", "uses transcript order between equals".
5. **A pick that asks for two** (the made-up program in `tests/fixtures/`: choose 2 of
   three). All three passed: the first two in order fill it, the third fills nothing
   ("pick already filled"), no pick open, 4 credits. One passed: one pick open, 2 credits
   done, 2 needed. One passed and one in progress, in either order: both fill it, one as
   done and one as in progress. — "fills a choose-2 pick with two courses".
6. **Not passed means not placed.** On ordinary rows (not transfer credit): ENG 131 with D
   and ENG 140 with W: the pick stays open, 0 credits, and neither is listed as filling or
   as left over. MKT 410 with D, with I, or with an unreadable grade: no elective place, 0
   elective credits. — "leaves a pick open
   when no course for it was passed", "gives no elective for a course that was not passed".
7. **Not in the curriculum, and transfer credit.** A passed course that is in no list and
   does not match the elective rule (BIO 230) fills nothing, with the reason "not in the
   curriculum", and adds no credits. Transfer credit is placed like any passed course:
   - a transfer row whose code is in no list and does not match the elective rule
     (BIO 230, grade column "T") fills nothing, with the reason "not in the curriculum";
   - a transfer row that matches the elective rule (MKT 410, "TR") fills an elective place
     as done, worth 3 credits;
   - a transfer row for a listed course fills that course's place as done, whatever its
     grade column shows: blank, "T", "TR" or a letter such as "D".
   — "does not count a course outside the curriculum", "places transfer credit like any
   passed course", "counts a transfer elective by the rule".
8. **The elective rule.** FIN 201 fills an elective place. So does any passed course whose
   subject is MGT, FIN, CIS, MKT, ACT or ECO and whose number is 300 to 499, unless it is
   listed in a block: MKT 410, CIS 499, MGT 300 and ACT 499 fill elective places; MGT 321,
   which is required, fills its own place and not an elective; MKT 299, ECO 500 and BIO 310
   do not match. Each is worth 3 credits from the curriculum, even if its row says 4. The
   subjects, the range and the credits are read from the curriculum data (shown with the
   made-up program, whose rule is different), and no pick course in the real data matches
   the rule. — "fills electives by the rule", "does not treat a required course as an
   elective", "reads the elective rule from the curriculum".
9. **Three electives, no more.** With four matching courses, three fill the places (passed
   before in progress, then transcript order) and the fourth fills nothing, with the reason
   "electives full"; elective credits never exceed 9. — "stops at three electives".
10. **Legacy-only courses are not electives here.** FLS 161, MGT 220 and MGT 203 passed
    fill nothing, with the reason "legacy-only elective" (never "not in the curriculum");
    PSY 251 fills the Social sciences pick like any other pick course and never an elective
    place. (F05 adds the legacy answer.) — "does not count legacy-only courses as electives".
11. **The sequence.**
    - HIS 131 + HIS 132 passed: done. ENG 209 + ENG 210 passed: done.
    - One alternative with both courses passed or in progress, and none complete: in
      progress.
    - Any other case: needed, and each alternative lists the courses still missing (HIS 131
      and ENG 209 passed: HIS 132, and ENG 210; HIS 131 passed and HIS 132 with D: HIS 132).
    - The sequence adds no credits.
    - HIS 131, then GEO 206, then HIS 132, all passed: HIS 132 fills the Social sciences
      pick as done; GEO 206 fills nothing ("pick already filled", by HIS 132); the sequence
      is done; Social sciences credits are the same as with GEO 206 alone.
    - The same for ENG 209 + HUM 101 + ENG 210: ENG 210 fills the second Humanities pick.
    - HIS 131 passed, GEO 206 passed, HIS 132 in progress: GEO 206 fills the pick as done;
      HIS 132 fills nothing and adds no in-progress credits; the sequence is in progress.
    — "completes the history or the literature sequence", "says which courses a sequence
    still needs", "places a sequence course in its pick", "keeps a sequence in progress when
    its course is running".
12. **The core blocks total 42 hours.** Proved on the data by `tests/curriculum.test.ts`
    ("carries the sequence alternatives inside the 42 core hours"). Here: a student who has
    passed everything in the core blocks (read from the curriculum's list of core blocks),
    including every alternative of every pick, has exactly 42 credits done there, not more.
    — "counts 42 credits for the complete core".
13. **Credits.** In every block, done + in progress + needed equals the block's credits;
    the same for the electives; the totals are the sums, and remaining = degree credits −
    done − in progress. Credits are the curriculum's: a row that says 4 credits for a
    3-credit course still counts 3; a withdrawn row counts nothing. A course in progress
    that fills nothing adds nothing to "in progress". — "adds credits up from the
    curriculum, not from the rows".
14. **Nothing, and everything.** An empty transcript: every place needed, 0 done, 121
    remaining, sequence needed. Every course listed in a block passed (all pick alternatives
    included) plus three rule-matched electives: 121 done, 0 in progress, 0 remaining,
    sequence done; the courses that fill nothing are exactly the unused pick alternatives,
    each with "pick already filled" and the course that fills it. — "handles an empty
    transcript", "reaches 121 with everything passed".
15. **The made-up student of the approved mockup.** Rows written out in the test: passed
    ORI 100, ENG 131, ENG 132, BIO 127, BIOL 127, BIO 128, BIOL 128, MAT 137, HIS 131,
    CIS 205, CIS 206; ECO 251 with a D and then a row in progress; in progress HUM 103,
    ACT 214, MGT 202, PED 114. Expected credits done / in progress / needed: Orientation
    1 / 0 / 0; Composition 6 / 0 / 0; Science & math 11 / 0 / 0; Social sciences 3 / 3 / 6;
    Humanities 0 / 3 / 9; Pre-professional 3 / 7 / 9; Business core 3 / 0 / 24; Major (CIS)
    0 / 0 / 24; CIS electives 0 / 0 / 9. Totals 27 / 13 / 81. The sequence is needed,
    missing HIS 132 for history, and ENG 209 and ENG 210 for literature. — "matches the approved
    mockup's student".
16. **Does not change what it is given**, and gives the same answer for the same input.
    — "does not change its input".
17. **The records are true.** `docs/decision-log.md` has the readings above (the order
    within a pick, the elective order, the sequence judged on courses, "not already
    required"), and `docs/sprints/_carried-forward.md` has a line for F14: pace is taken
    from this feature's totals, not from in-progress rows. — reviewer; the files.
18. **Made-up data only.** — the test file; reviewer.
19. **Refuses input it cannot place correctly** (added after QA round 1). `placeCourses`
    takes one standing per course with the code in its normal form, which is what
    `courseStandings` returns. The same course twice, or a code written another way
    ("mgt321", "MGT-321"), throws an error that names the course, instead of counting a
    course twice or losing it. — "refuses the same course twice, or a code that is not in
    its normal form".
20. **The sequence is separate from the credits** (added after the review). A student can
    have all 121 credits done while the sequence is still needed, when other courses hold
    the two picks; passing HIS 132 then completes the sequence, takes the pick and adds no
    credits. — "keeps the sequence apart from the credits".

21. **The two courses that never count** (added by the owner's answer of 2026-11-16).
    CIS 304 and CIS 415 passed, in progress or as transfer credit fill nothing, with the
    reason "not current", and take no elective place from courses that do count. CIS 303,
    CIS 305, CIS 414, CIS 416, CIS 399 and CIS 499 still fill elective places by the rule.
    The exceptions are read from the curriculum data (shown with the made-up program).
    — "does not count CIS 304 or CIS 415, on a transcript or as transfer credit", "still
    counts another 300/400-level CIS course that is not required", "reads the exceptions
    from the curriculum".

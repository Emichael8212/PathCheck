# F14 — Flow B pace: whether the student is on pace for their target semester, by credits only

Milestone: M1 · Branch: `feat/F14-pace` · Brief sections: §4 Flow B step 4

## What the student gets
Beside the credits left, the student sees whether they are on pace to finish by a target
semester, with the credits a term that takes. They can change the target, the fewest and
most credits a term and whether summers count, and the answer follows.

## User-visible change
Yes.

## What is built
On the result of "See what I have left", in the overview card of the approved design:

- **Beside the ring**: the label "Looking ahead", the answer ("On pace for Spring 2029"),
  one line of detail ("about 16 credits a term"), and always the caveat "Credits only —
  course order may add semesters."
- **Under the credit bar**: the label "Make the estimate yours" and the four controls of
  the design: "Target semester" (a list), "Fewest credits per term" and "Most credits per
  term" (− / +, starting at 12 and 18), "Summers" (a switch, off). The two notes of the
  design appear under their control: below 12, "Below 12 credits: below full-time; may
  affect scholarships."; over 18, "Over 18 credits: may cost extra." The two ends never
  cross (pushing one past the other takes it along); neither goes below 1, where its "−"
  is switched off; there is no upper limit.
- The settings are kept while the student moves between pages, for the visit.

## How pace is worked out (credits only)
Everything comes from the reviewed transcript and the settings. Nothing is taken from
today's date (owner, 2026-11-16).

- **Credits still to take** = the credits left of the checklist (the degree's total minus
  done minus in progress: a course in progress counts as passed), plus the extra credits
  of a sequence course when both sequence picks are held (owner's answer of 2026-11-16:
  the plan schedules it; so pace counts it).
- **The latest term on the transcript** = the latest term that has rows in progress; with
  none, the latest term with a graded row (the owner's rule for F15, 2026-11-16, applied
  here: the brief gives no other starting point, and it gives the design's numbers).
- **Reading a term**: a season and a four-digit year. "Fall"; "Spr" or "Spring"; "Sum" or
  "Summer". Letter case and extra spaces do not matter ("fall 2026", " Spr  2026 ": the
  student can type a term in the review). Rows of the transfer section are not looked
  at. The terms that decide are those of the rows in progress, or, with none, of the
  graded rows: when one of *those* cannot be read, pace is not estimated and the page
  says so. With rows in progress, an unreadable term on an older graded row changes
  nothing.
- **The terms ahead** start with the term after the latest one and go on in order: Spring,
  Fall, and Summer between them only when "Summers" is on.
- **Terms to the target** = how many of those terms there are up to and including the
  target semester.
- **Needed a term** = credits still to take ÷ terms to the target.
- **On pace** when needed a term is not more than "Most credits per term". One range for
  every term that is counted, summers included: the brief names one range, and PathCheck
  sets no limit of its own.
- **The default target** is the earliest term the student can finish in at "Most credits
  per term", so the first answer is always "On pace"; "Not on pace" appears when the
  student picks an earlier target or lowers the most. For the design's student: 81
  credits at 18 a term take 5 terms from Spring 2027, so Spring 2029, and 81 ÷ 5 is
  "about 16 credits a term". The list offers the term before the default, the default
  and the three after it, and the student's own choice if it is none of these. Until
  the student chooses, the target follows the settings.

## What the answer says
| Case | Answer | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| needed a term is between the fewest and the most | "On pace for <target>" | "about <n> credits a term" (rounded to a whole number) |
| needed a term is below the fewest | "On pace for <target>" | "At <fewest> credits a term you finish <term>, <k> semesters before your target." ("about <n> credits a term" when that is the target itself) |
| needed a term is more than the most | "Not on pace for <target>" | "At <most> credits a term you finish <term>, <k> semesters after your target." |
| nothing is left to take | "No credits left to plan" | none |
| the latest term cannot be read from the transcript | "Pace can’t be estimated: the latest term on your transcript could not be read." | none; the controls are not shown |

"one semester" / "2 semesters"; "term" for the range, "semester" for the target, as the
owner's own example for F15 has it ("at this pace you finish Fall 2029, one semester after
your target"). The caveat is under every answer of the first three rows.

## Decided here (recorded in `docs/decision-log.md`)
- **The starting term** is the owner's F15 rule applied to F14 (above).
- **On pace means reachable within the most credits a term.** The fewest changes the
  detail, not the verdict.
- **The default target** and the list of targets (above).
- **Summers use the same range** as Fall and Spring.
- **A summer may be chosen as the target** only while "Summers" is on; switching it off
  moves a summer target to the Fall after it.
- **While rows still need fixing**, the answer is given as above, and the note over the
  result says so of pace too: it ends "… The credits left and the pace may change too."
  (An unchecked row can only lower the credits left: a "Not on pace" may be untrue.)
- **The legacy question** still comes before the whole result, pace included (F13).
- **The Start demo's line** is worked out by this code for the demo's student instead of
  being written out: the student's courses are the examples file's (course codes only,
  as F06 requires, and unchanged), and the term they are in progress in is a line of the
  demo's own text in `src/start.ts`. Whatever the code says is what the demo shows.
- **Wording by the agent**, listed in the pull request: "Not on pace for <target>"; the
  two "At <n> credits a term you finish …" lines; "No credits left to plan"; the
  could-not-be-read sentence.

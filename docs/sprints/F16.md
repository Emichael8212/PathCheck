# F16 — Flow B draft plan: the remaining courses by semester, with Move later

Milestone: M1 · Branch: `feat/F16-draft-plan` · Brief sections: §4 Flow B step 5

## What the student gets
Under the checklist, the student sees one draft of the courses they still need, laid out
semester by semester, and can push any course to a later semester when the portal blocks
it. It is a starting point, and says so.

## User-visible change
Yes.

## What is built
On the result of "See what I have left", after the requirements, the "Draft plan" section
of the approved design:

- the label "A starting point, not a schedule", the heading "Draft plan", the dashed
  label "Draft — Order not checked — your portal checks prerequisites" and the line "Pace
  settings estimate credits only. “Move later” changes this draft; prerequisites are not
  checked.";
- one band for each semester: the term and its credits, and its entries. A course shows
  its code and title; a place the student still has to choose for shows "Open choice" and
  what it is for; every entry has "Move later";
- the course the curriculum names for the final semester (MGT 462) carries "Always the
  final semester".

Nothing can be dragged, adopted, previewed, applied or undone, and nothing checks
prerequisites or offerings (brief §6).

## How it is laid out
- **Order** (brief: "ordered by course level 100 → 400"): by the hundred of the course
  number, lowest first; within a level, in the curriculum's order. An open pick has the
  level of its lowest course; an open elective comes with the highest level of the
  elective rule; the extra sequence course with the level of its lower course. The
  final-semester course is always the last entry of the last semester.
- **Semesters** start with the first term ahead and follow the pace settings (F14): the
  same range and the same Summers switch. Each semester aims at the credits still to
  place ÷ the semesters left up to the end the pace answer implies (the target when on
  pace; where the most credits a term finish when not; where the fewest finish when that
  is before the target), kept between the fewest and the most.
- **Filling a semester**: it takes the entries that may go there, in order, each one
  that still fits under the most (one that does not fit is passed over for the next one
  that does), until it reaches its aim. An entry that was passed over, or moved, keeps
  its place in the order: it is the first entry of the semester it lands in, so the
  levels within a semester never go down. Courses cannot be split, so:
  - a semester is over the most only when it holds a single course that is itself bigger
    than the most (it then holds nothing else);
  - a semester is under the fewest only when it is the last, or when no entry that may
    go there still fits under the most;
  - a semester with no entry is not drawn (it can only come from moves).
- **The final-semester course** goes into the last semester after everything else, when
  it fits under the most; otherwise into a semester of its own after it. It is always
  the last entry of the last semester.
- **When the plan ends later than the pace answer implies** (a course did not fit; the
  final-semester course needed a semester of its own; the student moved courses), the
  plan says so under itself: "This draft ends in <term>, later than the pace estimate
  above: courses can’t be split across semesters." or "…: you moved courses later."
  (the second only when the plan without the moves would end earlier). When it ends at or before that term, nothing
  is said.
- For the design's student: Spring 2027 to Spring 2029 with 18, 18, 15, 15 and 15 credits,
  as the approved design shows.
- **Which course is the final-semester course** is named by the curriculum file (a new
  line there, `finalSemesterCourse`, from the brief's "MGT 462 Business Policies in the
  final semester"): no course code is written in the code, and the file's review date is
  not touched. A program whose file has no such line has no final-semester rule and no
  such note.

## Move later
- "Move later" on an entry moves it to the term after the one it is in; pressing again
  moves it one further. The other entries flow again in order into the room it left,
  under the same rules; the moved entry never comes back earlier.
- **What a move is kept as**: the entry's earliest term ("not before Fall 2027"). When the
  pace settings change, it still means that term: with Summers switched off, an entry
  kept for Summer 2028 goes no earlier than Fall 2028.
- From the last semester it opens a new one after it, and the final-semester course goes
  with the end: it is still the last entry of the last semester. Moving the
  final-semester course itself later puts it one term after the last semester, alone.
- **Which entry is which**: an open choice is its own place (the second elective place
  stays the second), so a move stays on the entry it was made on. Where several entries
  read the same, the button's name for a screen reader counts them: "Move later: CIS
  elective, 2 of 3". (The name starts with the words on the button, "Move later", so
  that saying them reaches it; changed from "Move MGT 321 later" after the code review.)
- What was moved is said to a screen reader ("MGT 321 moved to Fall 2027."; "CIS elective,
  2 of 3, moved to Fall 2029."), the keyboard
  stays on that entry's button, and nothing else on the page is drawn again.
- The moves are kept for the visit and forgotten with the transcript. Changing the pace
  settings keeps them.

## Decided here (recorded in `docs/decision-log.md`)
- **The curriculum file names the final-semester course.** The loader checks that it is a
  required course.
- **The order within a level, and where open choices and electives go** (above).
- **How credits are spread over the semesters** (above): the simplest rule that gives the
  design's 18, 18, 15, 15, 15.
- **Which pick names the sequence course**: the first open one in the curriculum's order.
  That is the Humanities pick in today's file (Humanities comes before Social sciences),
  which is what the design shows.
- **Where the plan differs from the screenshot** (the brief's order by level wins; listed
  in DESIGN.md and in the pull request): the open picks whose lowest course is 100-level
  come in the first semester, before the 200-level courses that the screenshot shows
  there; within a level the order is the curriculum's, not the screenshot's. The
  semesters, their credits and the 27 entries are the same. "Print / save PDF for your
  advisor" and "Clear my data", which end the design's page, are not drawn until F17 and
  F19.
- **No plan** when nothing is left ("No credits left to plan" beside the ring, F14) or
  when the latest term cannot be read (F14's sentence): the section is not drawn.
- **Rows still to fix**: the note above the result ends "The credits left, the pace and
  the draft plan may change too."
- **The legacy question** still comes before the whole result, the plan included.
- **Wording by the agent**, listed in the pull request: "<block> pick (… or another)";
  "HIS 132 or ENG 210, for the history or literature sequence"; "<code> moved to <term>.";
  the two "This draft ends in <term>, later than the pace estimate above: …" lines; the
  changed note above the result. The other words are the design's.
